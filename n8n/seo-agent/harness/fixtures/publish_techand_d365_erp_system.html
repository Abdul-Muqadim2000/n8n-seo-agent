Finance and IT leaders in the UAE often ask: is Microsoft Dynamics 365 an ERP system, or a CRM sold under the same name? This guide maps each app and gives a decision rule. It also covers UAE VAT, e-invoicing and data residency.

Quick answer: Yes. Microsoft Dynamics 365 is a family of cloud business applications, and several of them are ERP systems. Business Central is the ERP for small and mid-sized companies, while Dynamics 365 Finance and Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management serve larger, multi-entity organisations. Other apps in the suite, such as Sales and Customer Service, are CRM, and they connect to the ERP apps through Microsoft's Power Platform.

Is Microsoft Dynamics 365 an ERP system?

The confusion comes from Microsoft's branding. Dynamics 365 is not a single product that you install. It is a family of apps that are sold and licensed separately. Some apps run finance and operations, and others manage your customers. A 2026 overview from randgroup.com puts it plainly: "The short answer is: it is both."

Two product lines make up the ERP side, and both are enterprise resource planning systems in the full sense. Business Central is the packaged ERP for growing and mid-sized companies. Dynamics 365 Finance and Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management (F&SCM) form the enterprise ERP pair. They are two apps, licensed separately and usually deployed together. Partners may also call the pair Finance and Operations or F&O. If a proposal uses a name you cannot place, ask the vendor to map it to a specific Microsoft app.

Does Microsoft Dynamics have ERP?

Yes. Microsoft's current ERP products are Business Central and F&SCM. They cover financial management, project management and core business processes such as purchasing and inventory. They succeed the older Dynamics NAV, GP and AX products and run in Microsoft's cloud, with Power BI reporting and Copilot available inside the apps.

In practice, you do not buy "Dynamics 365" as a whole. You choose the ERP app that matches your entities and processes. You then add CRM or platform tools only where they solve a measurable problem, such as a slow quote-to-cash cycle or manual month-end reporting.

Is Dynamics 365 an ERP or a CRM?

It is both, delivered as separate apps. Business Central and F&SCM are the ERP apps for finance, inventory and operations. Sales, Customer Service, Field Service and Customer Insights are the CRM apps, which succeed Dynamics CRM. You license only the apps you need, and they share data through the Power Platform. Many UAE buyers therefore start with one ERP app and add Sales or Customer Service later.

Which Dynamics 365 products are ERP?

The ERP products are Dynamics 365 Business Central, Dynamics 365 Finance and Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management. Project Operations covers project financials and sits between ERP and CRM. A proposal that names Finance and Operations means the F&SCM pair, not a third product.

The platform layer: Power Platform, Power BI, Copilot and Azure

These tools are neither ERP nor CRM on their own. They sit underneath or alongside the apps:

  1. Power Platform: low-code apps, workflows and connectors for steps the standard apps do not cover.
  2. Power BI: reporting on business data from ERP and CRM in one place.
  3. Copilot and Copilot Studio: AI assistance inside the apps and custom agents, which add automation capabilities to routine finance and service tasks.
  4. Azure: Microsoft's cloud, which hosts the apps.
  5. Microsoft 365: Outlook, Teams and Excel, connected to your ERP records.

You can see the Dynamics 365 business applications we implement and how they fit together.

How did Dynamics AX, NAV, GP and Dynamics CRM become Dynamics 365?

Microsoft brought its separate ERP and CRM products under one cloud brand. This family line tells you where your current system is likely to land.

What happened to Dynamics AX, NAV and GP?

Microsoft Dynamics AX became Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management. Dynamics NAV became Business Central. For Dynamics GP, Microsoft documents a migration path to Business Central, so many GP customers assess Business Central first. Dynamics CRM became the customer apps, including Sales, Customer Service and Field Service. Check support dates for your exact version on Microsoft's lifecycle pages, not in a partner's summary.

The cloud apps change how you run the system day to day:

  1. Microsoft publishes release plans for regular updates. These replace the large version upgrades that older on-premises systems needed.
  2. Copilot works inside the apps, for example to summarise records or draft replies. Test it on your own tasks before you count on time savings.
  3. Power BI and the Power Platform read the same data as the ERP, so finance has fewer exports to reconcile.
  4. Users work from Outlook, Teams and Excel rather than switching between separate tools.

What makes a legacy migration harder or easier

Customisations and reports cause most of the effort. Business Central and F&SCM use extensions rather than changes to the base code, so old modifications need rework. We use five steps:

  1. Inventory every customisation, integration and report, with its owner and how often people use it.
  2. Classify each item: replace it with a standard feature, rebuild it as an extension, or retire it.
  3. Map each report to a standard report or to Power BI, and retire reports nobody opens.
  4. Cleanse master data and agree how many years of history to bring across.
  5. Run a parallel close: complete one month-end in both systems and reconcile the trial balance, VAT figures and stock before cut-over.

Illustrative example, not a client case: a distributor on a customised NAV system has a custom discount routine, a custom VAT report and two near-identical stock reports. The discount routine becomes a standard price list in Business Central. The VAT report is rebuilt as an extension. One stock report moves to Power BI and the other is retired. A parallel month-end then confirms the figures match before the old system is switched off.

In our projects, we deliver NAV and Dynamics GP migrations to Business Central and Microsoft Dynamics AX upgrades to F&SCM. Plan retraining too, because users have often relied on the old screens for years.

Business Central or Finance and Supply Chain Management: which fits your company?

This choice depends on your entity structure and process complexity, not on headcount alone. A trading group with modest headcount but many GCC entities may need more than a larger distributor trading from one UAE entity.

Which is better for a mid-size company: Business Central or Finance and Supply Chain Management?

Most mid-size companies with a few legal entities and standard trading or light manufacturing processes fit Business Central. Choose F&SCM if you run many entities across countries, need advanced consolidation, or have complex manufacturing or warehousing. It also suits companies with a heavy regulatory reporting load.

The UAE adds its own layer. Free zone and mainland operations often need separate legal entities, VAT treatment and bank accounts. GCC subsidiaries add further currencies and local tax rules.

A five-question self-check for UAE and GCC companies

  1. How many legal entities do you run across mainland, free zones and other GCC countries?
  2. Do you consolidate or trade between entities every month, and how much of that work is manual?
  3. How deep are your manufacturing and warehouse operations?
  4. What regulatory reporting do you carry beyond VAT?
  5. How much internal IT capacity can you commit, and how wide is your planned GCC rollout?

Our rule of thumb: if you answer "many", "complex" or "heavy" to two or more questions, shortlist F&SCM. Otherwise, start with Business Central.

Scored example, for illustration: a group with two mainland entities and one free zone entity answers "few", "light", "none", "VAT only" and "limited". That is no heavy answers, so Business Central goes on the shortlist. Add a Saudi manufacturing subsidiary with its own warehouses, and two answers turn heavy: entities and manufacturing. F&SCM then joins the shortlist.

Before we recommend either product, we run business discovery, requirements gathering, a gap-fit analysis and ROI assessment in our Assess and Envision phase.

What should UAE companies check before choosing Dynamics 365 ERP?

Is Dynamics 365 suitable for UAE VAT and multi-currency needs?

Yes, with checks. VAT set-up, multi-currency accounting and an AED base currency are standard ERP features. Microsoft documents localisation for Finance. Business Central's UAE localisation status appears on its country availability list. E-invoicing needs a connector to an Accredited Service Provider, not custom ERP code.

What is standard and what is not:

  1. Standard: VAT codes, VAT reporting, multi-currency, currency revaluation and bank reconciliation.
  2. Partner or provider connector: the link to your Accredited Service Provider for e-invoicing.
  3. Differs per app: Arabic interface and Arabic invoice layouts. Business Central's UAE localization and Arabic translation come from partner apps, while Finance prints UAE-layout sales invoices in two languages, the user's and the customer's (UAE invoice layout).

Take this checklist into every vendor meeting:

  1. VAT codes and returns. Ask how reverse charge and designated zone transactions map to the figures in your Federal Tax Authority VAT return.
  2. AED and multi-currency. Ask how AED base currency, revaluation and bank reconciliation are set up and tested for each GCC subsidiary.
  3. Arabic. Ask which apps produce Arabic invoices and offer an Arabic interface without custom work.
  4. Entity structure. Ask how the design models mainland, free zone and GCC entities and the transactions between them.

UAE e-invoicing and Peppol: what your ERP must do

The UAE model runs through providers accredited under the Ministry of Finance e-invoicing framework, which uses the Peppol network. Your ERP does not report to the authority directly. It sends structured invoice data to an Accredited Service Provider. Check current phase dates on the Ministry of Finance site, not in vendor slides.

Ask three questions:

  1. How does the ERP connect to the Accredited Service Provider?
  2. Who maintains the connector when Microsoft or the provider changes something?
  3. How do rejected invoices come back into the ERP for correction?

We deliver UAE e-invoicing (Peppol) and VAT compliance work alongside our Dynamics 365 projects. In those projects, we check that every VAT code feeds the correct return figure and test the provider connection end to end, including rejections.

Data residency, Azure regions and security

Microsoft lists its Azure geographies, which include UAE regions. Business Central environments localized for the UAE are hosted in the UAE Azure geography; for other Dynamics 365 apps, ask Microsoft which region hosts your tenant. Banking and financial services clients should involve their compliance team early, as sector rules may restrict hosting choices. See our security and data residency approach.

How does Dynamics 365 compare with SAP S/4HANA, Oracle and NetSuite for GCC buyers?

How does Dynamics 365 compare with SAP S/4HANA?

Both are full ERP platforms used by large, regulated and multi-site organisations. For GCC buyers, the practical differences are fit with your Microsoft 365 and Azure estate, the product range from mid-size to enterprise, the customisation approach and local partner depth. Run the same scripted demos with each vendor before deciding.

SAP S/4HANA, Oracle and NetSuite often share a shortlist with Dynamics 365. What separates them for your company:

  1. Microsoft fit: if you already run Microsoft 365 and Azure, Dynamics 365 shares the same identity, data and tools.
  2. Product range: Business Central covers mid-size needs and F&SCM covers enterprise ERP, so you can grow without changing vendor.
  3. Partner availability: check how many experienced consultants each product has in the UAE.
  4. Customisation approach: ask how each product keeps your changes intact through updates.
  5. AI: compare what Copilot and the alternatives actually do inside the apps.

Regulated banks and multi-site manufacturers: where each fits

A regulated bank uses ERP for the back office: general ledger, procurement, fixed assets and financial reporting. ERP does not replace core banking. F&SCM fits banks that already run Microsoft 365 and Azure and want finance inside the same identity and security model. SAP S/4HANA may be stronger where the group parent has standardised on SAP, or where group auditors already rely on SAP-based controls.

A multi-site manufacturer needs production, warehousing and planning across sites, which F&SCM covers. SAP may be stronger for large global manufacturers whose plants already run SAP, or whose industry processes are built around it. For a GCC manufacturer with a few plants and a Microsoft estate, F&SCM is worth testing first. Use your own bills of materials and routings in a scripted demo.

How to test our advice

We are a Microsoft partner, so weigh our view of SAP with that in mind. Ask us, and every partner, for a written fit rationale that ties each recommendation to your entities, processes and reports. If we recommend F&SCM, ask us why Business Central will not do. When Business Central, or no new ERP at all, is enough, we say so.

Questions to ask every ERP vendor on your shortlist

  1. How does your product connect to a UAE Accredited Service Provider, and who maintains that connector?
  2. Which consultants on your UAE team will work on our project?
  3. How are upgrades handled, and what happens to our customisations?
  4. Who owns support after go-live, and what response times apply?

Where ERP projects go wrong and how to avoid it

  1. Copying old customisations: you rebuild yesterday's workarounds and lose standard updates. Redesign against standard features first.
  2. Weak data cleansing: bad master data produces wrong VAT and stock figures from day one. Cleanse before you migrate.
  3. An under-resourced internal team: decisions stall. Release named owners from their day jobs.
  4. Late e-invoicing and VAT testing: invoices are rejected after go-live. Test with your provider before cut-over.
  5. No support owner: issues sit between the vendor and the partner. Agree support ownership before you sign.

You may not need ERP yet if...

Exit check: you may not need a new ERP yet if all of these are true.
  1. You run one legal entity and plan no new entity or GCC expansion in the next 12 months.
  2. Fewer than about 10 people use your finance system (our rule of thumb).
  3. Your current system files VAT returns without manual adjustments and can connect to an Accredited Service Provider.
  4. Month-end closes on time without spreadsheets rebuilding the figures.
  5. Nobody can be released to own an ERP project in the next 12 months.
What to do instead: confirm e-invoicing readiness in your current system, and add Power BI reporting on your existing data. Review the decision in 12 months or when you add an entity. If your busiest finance period is close, delay the start rather than disrupt your close.
