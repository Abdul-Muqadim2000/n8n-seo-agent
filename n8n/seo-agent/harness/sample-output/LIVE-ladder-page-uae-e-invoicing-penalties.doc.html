Is e-invoicing mandatory in the UAE?

Yes. It is mandatory for VAT-registered businesses in scope, on a phased timetable set by Ministerial Decision No. 244 of 2025. Voluntary adoption opens from 1 July 2026. Businesses with revenue at or above [[Price] million](https://worldcompanysetup.com/en/blog/uae-e-invoicing-mandate-dubai-timeline-penalties/) must appoint an ASP first, by 31 July 2026. They must go live from 1 January 2027.

When do e-invoicing penalties start, and which phase are you in?

Penalties start only once your deadline passes. Ministerial Decision No. 244 of 2025 sets two dates for each phase: an ASP appointment deadline and a go-live date. Ministerial Decision No. 243 of 2025 defines scope and exclusions. Confirm the dates below against the official decision before planning.

Are there penalties during the voluntary phase?

No. Cabinet Decision No. 106 of 2025 states that its provisions do not apply to businesses that issue, transmit or exchange e-invoices voluntarily. The safest time to test is therefore from 1 July 2026 until your mandatory date. Use that window to test your ERP, your ASP connection and your system-failure procedure without any fine exposure.

A three-question rule to find your deadline

  1. Are you VAT-registered and outside the exclusions in Ministerial Decision No. 243? If not, the mandate may not apply. Confirm this with your adviser.
  2. Is your revenue at or above [Price] million? If yes, the first-phase dates apply.
  3. Have you appointed an ASP? If not, your nearest exposure is the ASP deadline, not go-live.

What counts as a system failure, and what if your ASP goes down?

A system failure is a technical fault in your own system or your ASP's that stops you issuing or transmitting e-invoices. The decision requires you to notify the FTA within a set period and fines you if you do not. We have not confirmed that window or the amount, so take both from the MoF decision.

An ASP outage does not remove your own obligations. Do not assume your provider will notify the FTA for you. Check in the decision who must notify, then write that duty into your ASP contract.

System-failure incident checklist

  1. Detect and timestamp the failure, noting whether it sits in the ERP or the ASP.
  2. Raise a ticket with your ASP and record the reference.
  3. Notify the Federal Tax Authority within the required window and record the channel you used.
  4. Log the affected invoices, correspondence and screenshots, with detection, notification and resolution times.
  5. Re-transmit the backlog once the system failure is resolved, then reconcile.

What is an accredited service provider (ASP), and why does it matter for penalties?

An accredited service provider (ASP) is a provider accredited by the UAE authorities to exchange structured electronic invoices between businesses. It also reports invoice data to the FTA. Under UAE electronic invoicing, you cannot issue compliant e-invoices without an ASP. Missing your ASP appointment deadline triggers the [Price] monthly fine.

When you choose an ASP, check its accreditation status, PINT AE support, ERP connectors and uptime commitments. See the Peppol e-invoicing software features CFOs should look for.

Who is liable: the issuer, the recipient or the ASP?

As reported, the business carries the liability, not the service provider (ASP). Confirm the article for each violation in the MoF decision. UAE e-invoicing is commonly described as a five-corner model on Peppol and PINT AE. Data flows from the supplier to the supplier's ASP, then to the buyer's ASP and the buyer. The FTA is the fifth corner and receives the invoice data.

Issuers must issue and transmit each invoice and electronic credit note in the structured format on time. Recipients need an ASP and the ability to receive e-invoices. Commentary indicates they must also notify the issuer of failures. See what the FTA requires under UAE e-invoicing for the full obligations.

Your ASP is a service provider, so write SLAs, uptime targets and incident-reporting duties into the contract.

How do e-invoicing fines relate to VAT penalties, and can you appeal?

They are separate schedules and can apply together. You can also request reconsideration.

Late VAT payment vs e-invoicing fines

E-invoicing fines sit in Cabinet Decision No. 106 of 2025. General VAT penalties, including those for late payment, sit under Cabinet Decision No. 40 of 2017 as amended. A late electronic invoice can therefore attract both an e-invoicing fine and a separate VAT penalty. Take current late-payment rates from the FTA's published penalty schedule.

Requesting reconsideration from the FTA

Federal Decree-Law No. 28 of 2022 lets you ask the FTA to reconsider a penalty within 40 business days of being notified. If you disagree with the outcome, you can object to the Tax Disputes Resolution Committee. Your incident logs and ASP correspondence are your evidence, so keep them from day one.

Who is exempted from e-invoicing?

Ministerial Decision No. 243 of 2025 lists the excluded transactions and entities. Being VAT-registered does not by itself put every transaction in scope. Late VAT payment penalties fall under Cabinet Decision No. 40 of 2017 as amended, not under the e-invoicing decision.
