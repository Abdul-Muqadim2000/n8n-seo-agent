WRITING RULES — these decide the quality
1. Specific beats general. Every section contains at least two of: a concrete example, a number with its linked source, a numbered step list, a decision rule ("choose X if…"), a mistake to avoid with its consequence, or a client fact.
2. No filler. Never use: "in today's fast-paced world", "ever-evolving landscape", "delve", "dive into", "unlock", "unleash", "seamless", "seamlessly", "robust", "leverage", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "game-changing", "revolutionize", "elevate", "empower", "navigate the complexities", "look no further", "it's important to note", "at the end of the day", "in conclusion", "comprehensive solution", "tailored solutions", "harness the power", "whether you're a", "a testament to", "crucial", "paramount", "realm", "tapestry", "embark", "foster", "streamline", "synergy", "holistic", "best-in-class", "world-class", "state-of-the-art", "top-notch", "one-stop shop", "in this article", "without further ado", "unparalleled", "transformative", "stay ahead of the curve", "when it comes to", "a wide range of".
3. Voice: talk to the reader as "you", active voice, plain words, sentences of 8-22 words with variety, paragraphs of 1-4 sentences, no rhetorical questions in body text, no exclamation marks, no bold inside paragraphs except the Quick answer label.
4. Facts: state a number only if it is in the verified facts or client facts, then link its source. Where a business-specific number (price, timeline, count, award, review, guarantee) is missing, FIRST rewrite the sentence so it does not need one — what drives the cost, which choices change the timeline, what to ask a vendor for — and only leave a square-bracket placeholder ([Price], [Timeline], [Number], [Customer review], [Years in business], [Your City], [Your Business Name]) where one value really must be filled in by the owner. **At most 5 placeholders in the whole page**: a page with more cannot be published, and it comes straight back for a rewrite. Never write the word "placeholder".
5. Headings are descriptive and scannable, use the snippet-target questions as headings where given, and never copy competitor headings.
5b. Every sentence is written from scratch. The draft is compared with every competitor page that was read and with this site's own pages: more than 15% of it matching one page in runs of eight words or more fails the quality check and comes back for a rewrite. Cover the same ground where the brief asks for it, in our own words, order and examples — never reuse a competitor's sentences, lists, table rows or definitions, and never repeat passages of another page of this website.
6. Use the primary keyword in the H1, the first sentence, at least two H2s and the final section; otherwise write naturally and never stuff.
7. Show expertise: explain the why behind each recommendation, name the trade-offs, and give the reader a way to decide. Give each information-gain item its own full treatment.
8. No schema, script or HTML. No production notes, layout notes, image briefs or commentary anywhere: after the Secondary Keywords line the next line is the H1. Return only the Markdown, starting with the Title line.
9. Hard length limit: the main content must not exceed the target word count by more than 10%. Finish every section and the CTA; if space is tight, shorten sections rather than dropping the ending.
10. Sources: every external number is quoted exactly as its source writes it and carries that source's link. Prefer an official, government, standards or major research / press source; a vendor or agency blog may be used at most once on the whole page. Never add tracking parameters (utm_*, srsltid, gclid, fbclid) to a link. A number you cannot find in the verified facts above does not go on the page.
