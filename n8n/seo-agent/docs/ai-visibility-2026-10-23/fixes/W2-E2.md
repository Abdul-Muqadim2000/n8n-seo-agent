## 1. Intervals that respect clustering (`ci_json`)

**Method chosen: design effect + Korn-Graubard** (`AVX.prop`). For a rate: the cluster-robust variance over QUESTIONS (every answer to one
question, across engines and pulse mornings, is one cluster) divided by the binomial variance = the design effect, floored at 1; Wilson on
n_eff = n / deff, shrunk by (z / t_{questions-1})^2 so a small panel is not read as if its between-question variance were known. A rate of 0% or
100% has no variance of its own: its design effect is borrowed (the median rho of the competitors' named rates and the cited rate in the same
answers; `basis: 'borrowed'`), and with nothing to borrow it assumes rho = 1 (`'assumed'`, the widest honest range). Shares and means (share of
voice, mean place, position-weighted visibility, net sentiment) use the linearised ratio estimator over question totals times t_{q-1} (`AVX.ratio`).

Measured (harness W2E2-SIM, beta-binomial data with a known rate; seeded, so reproducible):

| panel | rate | rho | runs | **design effect (shipped)** | plain Wilson (old) | width shipped / plain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 q x 7 answers (reference) | 30% | 0.3 | 2,000 | **95.3%** | 75.3% | 16.2 / 9.5 pts |
| 8 q x 5 | 30% | 0.3 | 1,000 | 95.4% | 81.2% | 43.9 / 26.5 |
| 20 q x 7 | 30% | 0.3 | 1,000 | 95.1% | 76.8% | 25.8 / 14.8 |
| 50 q x 7 | 10% | 0.3 | 1,000 | 94.7% | 76.4% | 10.7 / 6.2 |
| 50 q x 7 | 50% | 0.5 | 1,000 | 94.7% | 64.7% | 21.0 / 10.4 |
| 50 q x 7 | 30% | 0.02 | 1,000 | 95.2% | 91.4% | 10.5 / 9.5 |
| 20 q x 3/5/12 (unequal) | 25% | 0.4 | 1,000 | 93.7% | 65.3% | 29.2 / 14.5 |
| 500 q x 9 | 20% | 0.3 | 300 | 97.0% | 77.7% | 4.3 / 2.3 |

The old interval under-covers badly wherever answers cluster (65-81%); our "±3 points at 500 questions" (AV-G6 §4) was optimistic by about
half. Alternatives measured in the scratch run of the same simulation: the two-level bootstrap (questions, then answers) covered 93-99% (95.5% at
20 q in the harness), the one-level cluster bootstrap under-covered at small panels (90.0% at 8 q). **Speed** (W2E2-SPEED, n8n's Node in the
container): the closed form takes **35 ms** on 8,982 answers (500 q x 9 engines + a sampled pulse); a two-level bootstrap of B = 1,000 on 9,000
answers took 182 ms. Both are far under 2 s; the closed form was chosen for its coverage at small panels (8 questions: 95.4% vs the one-level
bootstrap's 90%) and because it gives the design effect the change tests need. The whole W2-E2 measurement (intervals, 36,000-answer 28-day window,
share of voice, 10 impact bootstraps, rules) took **681 ms** per site at that size.

**Correction (R-E10, 2026-10-23, `REVIEW_ENGINE.md`):** the 681 ms above was one quiet reading, not a property of the code. The review's head run
measured **2,903 ms** for the same work (3,055 ms in all) on the same laptop under load and failed the 2,000 ms bar; the fixer's runs measured 531 /
548 ms (median of three: 758, 548, 527 ms; the closed-form intervals 25-28 ms) with a CPU calibration loop of **184 ms** in the same process, i.e.
**about 3 calibration loops** of work. The gate now takes the median of three runs and holds it against that loop measured in the same process
(pass: under 2,000 ms, or under 10 loops and 15 s on a loaded machine; the intervals under 200 ms, or under 1 loop and 2 s). The real cost on the
production e2-medium VM is not measured here; read it as a few seconds per 500-question site, not "681 ms".

`ci_json` = `{ method: 'design-effect', level: 95, headline: { named_rate, cited_rate, first_rate: { rate, lo, hi, k, n_answers, n_questions, deff, rho,
df, n_eff, basis, why, wilson_naive }, share_of_voice, pos_visibility, avg_position: { rate, lo, hi, n_answers, n_questions, df, basis } }, by_engine,
rho_lent, questions, answers, ms, note }`. `mention_lo / mention_hi` (row), `metrics.mention_ci`, `citation_ci`, `sov_ci` and each engine's `ci`
take the clustered range when it is measured (the plain one is kept as `ci_unclustered` / `wilson_naive`).

**The change tests too.** The named and cited week-on-week tests now use the pooled variance times each run's design effect (this run's; the
earlier run's from `metrics_json.deff`, stored from now on). Effect seen in the harness: F1B-3's old fixture (questions 0-5 always name you, 6-9
never) went from "real change, p 0.007" to "within noise" - correct: those 40 answers carry the information of about 10 questions. `tests.*.z_unclustered`
keeps the old z. The fixtures F1B-3 and S164 were moved accordingly (F1B-3 spreads its named answers over the questions; S164 asserts "clears 95%
alone" on the unclustered z).

**rule_version: not bumped.** No headline figure's definition changed (named / cited rates, share of voice, place and score are computed as before);
what changed is the width of their ranges and whether a change is called real. A comparable earlier row is still compared; its missing
`deff` is read as this run's.

## 2. The 28-day window for segments

Each weekly row now stores **`units_json`**: this run's weekly answers one by one (compact: questions and engines indexed once; 145 KB for a
4,500-answer week). A segment's level is counted over the last 28 days: this run's answers + earlier weekly rows' `units_json` + the pulse
mornings' `detail_json`, each reading once, current measurement rule only (older rules and rows without a per-answer record are left out and
counted in `window_28d.left_out`). Every segment row gets `w28 = { rate, lo, hi, n, k, questions, deff, basis, enough }`; `segments_json` gains the
columns `rate_28d, lo_28d, hi_28d, n_28d, k_28d, q_28d, deff_28d` and `window_28d` (from, to, weekly_runs, pulse_days, answers, left_out, note).
**The segment's change is still tested run against run** on non-overlapping answers (the run-window `n` / `k`): two overlapping 28-day windows
share three weeks of answers and a z-test between them would be wrong. The headline keeps the run's window. The report prints the 28-day rate
with its range, answers, questions and "28 days", and the change column as before.

## 3. Share of voice over a declared set (`sov_json`)

`ai_sov_set` (comma list; Site Admin `monitors`) or, when empty, the configured competitors - canonicalised with the run's own entity keys. Mention
share = mentions of each entity / all brand mentions (one per answer per business), over the answers whose businesses were recorded; every
business outside the set counts in **others** (with its top names), so a new rival cannot silently shrink the share. Ranges: ratio estimator
over questions. Both denominators: all answers and answers naming any business (`named_rate_all`, `named_of_branded`, each with its range and n).
**Mean normalised position** = 1 - (rank - 1) / (businesses named - 1), averaged over the answers that name you (1 when you are the only one), with
n, questions and range. Shape as the contract plus `set_source`, `others_lo/hi/n/top`, `mentions`, `denominators.answers_recorded`, `note`.

## 4. Sentiment (`sentiment_json`)

*Sentiment Jobs* sends **every** answer of the run that names the business (this execution's parsed answers with their full text, and the ones
earlier executions of a split run stored) to *Sentiment Reader* (Claude Haiku 4.5), 20 passages a call, at most 40 calls (800 answers; the rest
are counted as `unread` with the reason). The passage is the sentences about the business (`SENT.snippet`; W2-E1's `answer_text` when stored,
else the parser's in-memory text, else the excerpt). Per answer: polarity, aspects (price, support, integration, trust, quality, ease, features,
other) each with a polarity, the objection, the polarity toward each rival named. Net = (positive - negative) / answers read, with a range over
questions; withheld below 12; per aspect and per rival net under the same floor; objections with counts and engines.

**The labelled set** `harness/accuracy/ai_sentiment.json`, 256 snippets: 66 tuning (written by W2-E2), 70 hold-out round 1 and 40 real (from the
repo's stored DataForSEO answers) written by a labelling agent **before any classifier existed** (commit `fe94172f`), 80 hold-out round 2 written
blind by a second labelling agent after the rules were frozen (rules frozen in `2784eb20`, before the set was scored).

| rule reader, polarity Cohen's kappa | round 1 (built on T only) | round 2 (improved on H + R) |
|---|---|---|
| hold-out H (70, blind in round 1) | **0.587** | 0.956 (seen) |
| real R (40) | 0.697 | 0.851 (seen) |
| **blind hold-out K (80, read once)** | - | **0.548** (accuracy 70%) |
| all 256 | - | 0.827 |

**The rules do not pass 0.7 on blind data**, so they never make a published figure: only the model's labels are counted; the rules are a second
opinion whose agreement with the model is printed each run (`model_vs_rules`). What the harness gates (W2E2-SENT): the rules over all 256 cases
>= 0.7 and the blind hold-out as a ratchet (>= 0.548, may not fall); the kit's stated kappas equal the measured ones; the **parser** on the stored
expected model outputs (`ai_sentiment_model_fixtures.json`, 13 jobs) reproduces every label (polarity and aspect kappa 1.0) and survives fenced,
wrapped, mislabelled and refused outputs. **What is NOT gated: the model itself** - the harness cannot call Claude. `accuracy/eval_sentiment_model.js`
scores it with a key (256 short Haiku calls, about $0.10; not run). Until its hold-out kappa is written into `SENT.KAPPA.model`, `sentiment_json`
says `validated: false` and the report says "not yet validated". Not built: a majority of 3 model reads on borderline cases (one read per answer;
disagreement with the rules is counted instead).

## 5. Did an action work (`impact_json`)

Actions: AI targets written / won (`seo_ai_targets`, its `prompt_id`) and pages published (`seo_content_log`, matched to the panel's questions by
their search phrase), in the last 31 days (their 28 days before must be inside the 60 days loaded). Treated = the action's questions; controls =
questions no action touched within 56 days, same topic cluster first, then the same buyer stage, closest "before" rate, at least 3 per treated
question. Named rate 28 days before vs after (from the weekly `units_json` and the pulse records); difference in differences with a seeded
**two-level bootstrap** (questions resampled within each arm, then each question's answers per period), B = 2,000; "likely helped" (range above 0),
"likely hurt" (below 0), "can't tell" otherwise, under 12 answers in any cell, or under 7 days after; the words "not proof of cause" are in every
verdict. Harness W2E2-DID: planted +50 points -> likely helped (+51.6, 41.8 to 61.7); no effect -> can't tell (+1.2, -7.4 to 10.3); planted fall ->
likely hurt; 3 days after -> too soon; end to end through AI Metrics with a target and a published page. Not built: corrections of wrong claims
(their outcome is the claim disappearing, a different measure - open item).

## 6. Owner alert rules (`ai_alert_rules_json` -> `alerts_rules_json`)

Site Admin `monitors` accepts `ai_alert_rules_json` (string or list; alias `ai_alert_rules`) and `ai_sov_set`; Admin Action validates with the
engine's own `AVX.validateRules` (metric from the contract's six, scope keys engine / cluster / persona / market / competitor, one segment at a
time, competitor_share needs a competitor, direction drop / rise / any, 0 < threshold <= 100, unique ids, at most 25) and refuses a bad rule with
its reason; Monitor Row (Admin) stores both (exact columns); Monitor Row (Track) keeps them as stored. Evaluation: only on a comparable run
(`compare_json.comparable`); headline metrics read the `compare_json` test; a segment rule tests named / cited on that segment's own answers with
the run's design effect (other metrics per segment carry no test and say so); competitor_share reads the competitor's share test. The rules that
have a test are Holm-corrected together (5%), and a rule fires only when its test passes AND |delta| >= its threshold AND the direction matches;
fired rules join the run's alerts (the existing alert path). `alerts_rules_json` = `[{ rule_id, fired, metric, scope, direction, threshold_points,
delta, test: { z, p, threshold_p, method, tested_rules, passed }, reason }]`.
